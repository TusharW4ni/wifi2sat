WINDOW EXPERIMENT · diagnostic κ not recoverable
Internal diagnostic · Phases 0–3

Why the free-hand data can't measure the window δtmax

The window experiment was built to measure one number — the geometry-decorrelation curvature κ, and from it the usable window δtmax. On the data we have, we can't. This is the honest account of why: three compounding failures, with the numbers, and the concept confusions they create.

Scope: diagnosis only — what broke and why, not what to do about it.

Bottom line up front

On the free-hand data, the core thesis — that a hand perturbs GNSS carrier phase in a geometry-dependent way we can turn into a decorrelation window — is not demonstrable. Not because the theory is wrong (κ checks out on synthetic ground truth), but because the data breaks the measurement at three compounding points:

① the carrier-phase signal doesn't reproduce (α ≈ 0); ② the only thing that does reproduce is amplitude (CN0) — the wrong channel for κ; ③ any apparent decay is confounded with acquisition time, not geometry — and the clean cross-day test comes back at chance.

Net: κ is unidentifiable and δtmax is currently unmeasured. The barrier is data reproducibility and experimental confounding — not the math.

1 The target

What we set out to measure

After single-differencing and detrending, one satellite's cleaned gesture signal is a dot product, si = gi·d(t) — the sensitivity vector times the hand motion. Repeat the same gesture a bit later and the sky has drifted, so g rotates and the two signals stop matching. The theory says that mismatch grows quadratically:

r(δt) ≈ α·(1 − ½ κ ωLOS² δt²)

  • κ — the decorrelation curvature: how fast the sky wrecks the signal. A property of the gesture's shape. This is the number the experiment exists to measure.
  • α — the reproducibility floor: the correlation at zero drift (how well the gesture repeats at all).
  • δtmax = ωLOS−1√(2(1−rmin)/κ) — turn κ into the usable window.

Measuring κ means fitting the curvature of r as geometry drifts. Everything below is about why that fit is impossible on the current data.

2 The measurement chain

Three things must be true to read κ off data

To fit the decay curve and extract κ, a chain of preconditions has to hold — in order. If any link breaks, κ is unidentifiable. In the free-hand data, all three break.

3 The evidence

The three compounding failures

① The phase signal doesn't reproduce (α ≈ 0)

Phase 1 (alpha_study.py) measured reproducibility α across all sessions, five channels, every gesture, against a matched null with bootstrap CIs. The result for the channels κ actually lives on — the carrier-phase channels — is stark: they sit at the null.

  • Single-difference (SD), common-mode residual (CMR), common-mode (CM) carrier phase: α ≈ 0 across sessions.
  • push-SD is the one exception, and only barely: α ≈ 0.13–0.24, and only for push.
  • star — the high-κ gesture whose decorrelation would be the whole point — is indistinguishable from noise.

② The only reproducible signal is amplitude — the wrong channel

The one channel that reproduces is CN0 — signal strength — with α up to 0.74. But CN0 is a broad, non-directional proximity / shadowing effect: "a hand is nearby, partially blocking the sky." κ is a theory about the phase trajectory's geometry — the directional g·d projection. Amplitude has no κ.

Phase 2 (separability.py) nails this with an ablation on c1.1_day1 (5-class, chance 20%):

  • CN0-only ≈ CN0+SD ≫ SD-only (at chance).
  • So the class-separating information is CN0 amplitude, not phase. The phase channel adds essentially nothing.
what this meansEven the signal we can build on is the wrong kind to extract κ from. A geometry-decorrelation curvature is defined on the phase-projection mechanism; measuring it off an amplitude proximity effect is a category error.

③ The decay is confounded with time, not geometry

This is the one that would sink the measurement even if ① and ② were fixed. Gestures were recorded in contiguous per-gesture time-blocks, so within a session, window separation ≡ elapsed time ≡ geometry drift all move together (collinear). Any decay you see could be geometry (κ) or the room drifting over minutes — the design can't tell them apart. Two controls expose it:

Phase 3, Arm 1 (different geometry, same day): CN0 accuracy appears to decay with window separation — but the gesture-free baseline reproduces the same decay. So it's a time/environment effect, not attributable to geometry.

Phase 3, Arm 2 (same geometry, different day — the decisive test): revisit the same sky one sidereal day later, so geometry is held fixed while time/environment change. If the signal were geometry-locked it must transfer. It doesn't — day1→day2 collapses to chance (W2 CN0 33% vs 30% baseline; W3 and SD at chance). There is no transferable, geometry-locked fingerprint to attach κ to.

4 Clearing up the concepts

Three distinctions that keep tripping people up

These come up every time the results are discussed. Getting them straight is the difference between reading the failure correctly and mis-diagnosing it. (What to do about any of this is a separate proposal — out of scope for this diagnostic.)

κ vs α — the geometry clock vs the starting line

These are different animals and constantly get merged:

 what it measuresdepends onin our data
κhow fast the sky wrecks the signal (gesture shape)nobody — it's physicsunmeasurable (no signal to fit)
αhow well a performer repeats the gesturethe performer (rig ≈ 0.85, hand ≈ 0.6)≈ 0 for phase

Why it matters here: our headline problem — phase α ≈ 0 — is an α problem, a reproducibility failure. It says nothing about whether κ (the geometry mechanism) is real or not. The mechanism could be perfectly intact and we would still see nothing, because α gates whether κ is observable at all. Low α doesn't disprove the physics; it hides it.

Does the "mechanical window" transfer to the hand? Same clock, different start

A natural worry: if a machine measured a window, would it apply to a human hand? Split the falloff — r = α·(geometry term) — and it resolves cleanly. The rate of geometric decorrelation, as a fraction of where it starts (r/α = 1 − ½κΔθ²), does not depend on the performer. κ is the same for hand and machine.

Humans never need α > r_min — the product isn't template matching

The last confusion: "how do we get people to repeat gestures well enough (α) to clear the quality bar (rmin)?" You don't, and you never need to. That target only exists for a template-matcher comparing a live gesture to a stored one. The eventual product recognizes gestures with a classifier trained across geometry — it learns "star-ness" despite human variability by having seen that variability. So a low human α is not a product blocker.

the cruxLow human α is a measurement blocker (it hides κ from us right now), not a product blocker (a classifier doesn't care). Keeping "measuring the physics" and "building the product" separate dissolves most of the confusion these results generate.
5 The state of the number

So what is the window right now?

Unmeasured. Unknown. No measured κ → no empirical δtmax. For the carrier-phase-geometry mechanism, we do not have a value.

  • The numbers in the interactive lecture/calculator are not measurements. They come from an assumed / synthetic κ (verified in geomlib simulation), shown to illustrate the scaling. Do not read "star ≈ 90 min" as a result — it isn't one.
  • The only real evidence bearing on "how long" points short. The one reproducible signal (amplitude) fails to transfer across a single day at matched geometry. So the practical lifetime of the current signal is under a day, and it's environment-driven — not a clean geometric window.
say it preciselyIt is not "δtmax is large and we just haven't measured it." It's "we have a formula and a simulation that say a window should exist and be gesture-shape-dependent, but zero empirical value from real data — and what little real evidence we have says the current signal doesn't even last a day."
6 Honest bounds

What this does — and doesn't — prove

  • Small N throughout (≤6 reps/gesture/window; cross-day tests n = 6–30). A small real transferable effect cannot be excluded — only that none is demonstrable here.
  • This is about this free-hand dataset, not the physical hypothesis in principle. The κ mechanism is verified on synthetic ground truth (geomlib): the math is sound. The failure is the data's reproducibility floor and confounding, not the theory.
  • The amplitude (CN0) effect is real within a session. It's the transfer and the phase/geometry attribution that fail — not the existence of some hand-driven signal.
  • The pipeline is not the problem. Phases 0–3 (loader, slip-cleaning, common-reference SD, onset alignment, α study, separability, coherence arms) are built and validated; they're what let us see these failures clearly.
