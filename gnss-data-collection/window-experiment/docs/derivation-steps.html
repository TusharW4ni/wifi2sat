Geometry-decorrelation curvature κ
and the gesture window δtmax

The full chain from the carrier-phase signal model to the usable time window, one step at a time — clean math, terse captions. A hand near a fixed GNSS antenna perturbs carrier phase; do the same gesture later and the sky has drifted. This derives exactly how fast that drift erases reproducibility, and why single-axis gestures shrug it off while multi-axis gestures decorrelate quadratically.

Canonical text: THEORY.md  ·  Interactive version: derivation-explainer.html

01 Setup & signal model

What the receiver records

A hand acts as a moving multipath reflector near a fixed antenna. Let d(t)∈ℝ³ be the hand displacement (the gesture), êi the line-of-sight unit vector to satellite i, and λ the carrier wavelength. To leading order the phase perturbation is the projection of the motion onto the LOS:

φi(t) ≈ 2πλ êi·d(t) + c(t) + ρi(t) + noise gesture term  +  receiver clock c(t)  +  geometric range trend ρi(t)  +  noise

The 2π/λ converts a path-length change into radians. Three of the four terms are nuisances; the next step removes two of them exactly.

02 Single-difference & detrend

Peel away the clock and the range trend

Single-differencing against a reference satellite subtracts c(t) — common to all satellites, so it cancels exactly. A low-order detrend over the short window removes the smooth ρi(t). What remains is the clean gesture signal:

si(t) = gi·d(t) gi = 2πλ (êi − êref) clean signal = differential LOS sensitivity vector · hand displacement

gi is the differential LOS sensitivity vector: everything about the sky is baked into it, everything about the gesture into d(t). All geometry enters through the gi.

03 Correlation under drift

Compare two repetitions

Perform the same d(t) at times A and B separated by Δt. In between the LOS rotates, so gA → gB. Reproducibility is the normalized zero-lag correlation over the window (amplitude-invariant: r=1 for a rescaled copy):

r = ⟨sA, sB⟩ ‖sA‖ ‖sB‖ = ∫ (gA·d)(gB·d) dt √∫ (gA·d)2 dt · √∫ (gB·d)2 dt

Integrals run over the gesture window [0, Tg]. Next we collapse each integral into a matrix quadratic form.

04 Structure matrix M

The generalized cosine in the M-metric

The g-vectors are constant, so they slide out of the time integral, and the gesture collapses into one 3×3 second-moment (scatter) matrix:

∫ (gA·d)(gB·d) dt = gA⊤ (∫ d d⊤ dt) gB = gA⊤ M gB
M = ∫0Tg d(t) d(t)⊤ dt the structure matrix — the gesture's shape frozen into one 3×3 matrix

Every inner product becomes a quadratic form in M, so the correlation is the generalized cosine between the two geometry vectors, measured in the metric M:

r = gA⊤ M gB √gA⊤ M gA · √gB⊤ M gB

All gesture shape is in M; all geometry drift is in gA→gB. The rest of the derivation watches this cosine fall as the sky rotates.

05 Small-drift expansion

Taylor-expand the cosine to second order

Write gB = gA + Δg with Δg small, and name the three quadratic pieces:

a = g⊤Mg b = g⊤MΔg c = Δg⊤MΔg a is order 1 · b is order Δθ · c is order Δθ²

The numerator is a+b; the denominator's second factor is √(a+2b+c). Divide top and bottom by a and set β=b/a, γ=c/a:

r = a+b√a √a+2b+c = 1+β√1+2β+γ

Taylor-expand the root with √(1+x) ≈ 1 + x/2 − x²/8, multiply out, and keep terms to Δθ². The order-Δθ terms cancel exactly:

√1+2β+γ ≈ 1 + β + γ2 − β22 r ≈ (1+β)(1 − β − γ2 + 3β22)
r ≈ 1 − 12 (γ − β2) ⟹ 1 − r ≈ 12 c a − b2a2

Decorrelation is quadratic in the drift. The linear term vanishing is what makes the leading behavior a curvature.

06 The curvature κ

Substitute the drift direction → the closed form

The LOS rotates by a tiny angle Δθ, so Δg = Δθ w, where w = dg/dθ is fixed by the geometry. Then b=Δθ(g⊤Mw) and c=Δθ²(w⊤Mw); the Δθ factors pull out front:

r ≈ 1 − 12 κ Δθ2 κ = (w⊤Mw)(g⊤Mg) − (g⊤Mw)2 (g⊤Mg)2 κ is dimensionless — the decorrelation curvature

Correlation starts at 1 and falls off along a parabola whose steepness is a single number κ, built only from gesture shape M and geometry g, w.

07 Properties of κ

κ ≥ 0, and κ = 0 for a single axis

Non-negativity (Cauchy–Schwarz in the M-inner-product)

The numerator of κ is exactly the Cauchy–Schwarz gap in the M-metric:

(g⊤Mw)2 ≤ (g⊤Mg)(w⊤Mw) ⟹ κ ≥ 0.

Drift can only decrease correlation — never increase it.

κ = 0 ⇔ M is rank-1 (single-axis gesture)

If the gesture moves along a fixed direction n̂, then d(t)=f(t) n̂ and M collapses to rank 1:

M = (∫ f2 dt) n̂ n̂⊤ the cigar collapsed to a needle

For any vector x, Mx = (∫f²)(n̂·x)n̂ — always along n̂. So Mg ∥ Mw, and the two numerator terms become identical and cancel:

κ1D = (∫f²)²[(n̂·w)2(n̂·g)2 − (n̂·g)2(n̂·w)2] (∫f²)²(n̂·g)4 = 0
Why it makes sense A 1-D gesture senses only the scalar n̂·g. As geometry drifts, that scalar changes the signal's amplitude but never its shape f(t) — and correlation is amplitude-invariant, so r stays pinned at 1.
08 Multi-axis ⇒ κ > 0

Every extra dimension shrinks the window

If d(t) explores two or three dimensions (a star, a circle), M is full-rank with spread eigenvalues, Mg and Mw are generally not parallel, and:

rank(M) > 1 ⟹ Mg ∦ Mw ⟹ κ > 0.

The signal shape — not just amplitude — changes with geometry, so correlation falls quadratically. The larger the eigenvalue spread of M, the larger κ and the tighter the window. The two probe gestures sit at the κ-extremes:

gesturecharacterM eigenvalues (norm.)κwindow
pushnear single-axis[1, 0.009, 0.008] (≈ rank-1)≈ 0wide
stargenuinely multi-axis[1, 0.34, 0.28]> 0tight (binding)

Eigenvalues are illustrative; the operative values are the empirical M measured from recovered data.

09 The usable window

Turn κ into seconds: δtmax

Fix a minimum acceptable correlation rmin, set the parabola equal to it, and solve for the tolerable drift:

rmin = 1 − 12κ Δθmax2 ⟹ Δθmax = √2(1 − rmin)κ

The differential LOS rotates at rate ωLOS, so Δθ = ωLOS δt, giving the headline formula:

δtmax = 1ωLOS √2(1 − rmin)κ the window shrinks like κ−1/2

δtmax ∝ κ−1/2: push (κ→0) → window limited by reproducibility, not geometry; star (finite κ) → finite window. Here ωLOS ≈ 0.45°/min for GPS/BeiDou-MEO (set by orbital rate, nearly independent of elevation).

10 Reproducibility floor

Hands aren't machines: the floor α

At zero geometry drift, two free-hand repetitions still are not identical: r(Δθ=0) = α < 1, the reproducibility floor, bounded above by the thermal/carrier-phase noise ceiling rmax ≈ 0.85. The combined model multiplies the two effects:

r(δt) ≈ α (1 − 12κ ωLOS2 δt2) repeatability × geometry falloff

Free-hand α ≈ 0.6 — well below the ~0.85 ceiling — buries the gentle geometry dip. Isolating κ therefore requires mechanically reproduced gestures.

11 Sidereal repeat

Ask the sky to repeat itself

Rather than wait for natural drift, revisit the geometry. GPS/BeiDou-MEO geometry recurs every sidereal day:

Tsid = 86164.0905 s ≈ solar day − 3 m 56 s GPS repeats in 1 sidereal day; GPS+BeiDou-MEO jointly every 7 sidereal days

Collect a reference at T, revisit at T + Tsid + ε for a set of offsets ε. The induced drift is Δθ = ωLOS ε, so sweeping ε traces r(Δθ) directly — compared against the predicted 1 − ½κΔθ².

12 Result in one line

The headline

The whole derivation, compressed

The reproducible signal is si=gi·d; correlation under drift is the M-metric cosine of the geometry vectors, and it falls quadratically as r ≈ 1 − ½κΔθ².

Single-axis gestures are geometry-robust (κ→0, rank-1 M); every extra dimension shrinks the window like κ−1/2. Gesture shape, via the rank / eigenvalue-spread of M, sets everything — and a reproducibility floor α near the noise ceiling is what makes the geometric κ measurable at all.
